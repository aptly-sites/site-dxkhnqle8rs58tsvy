GEORGIA RESIDENT HANDBOOK

At home with All 3 Realty.

Guidance from your resident handbook, from picking up the keys to moving out.

Moving in

Your first three business days

Set up utilities and trash

Transfer utilities into your name within three business days of move-in and send the account numbers to your leasing specialist. The handbook lists a $50 fee beginning on day four, plus charges incurred. Arrange trash collection directly.

Record the condition of your home

Your leasing agent uploads the inspection to your portal before move-in. Add any overlooked items within 48 hours of your lease start. The inspection records the condition you accepted at move-in. Submit repair requests separately. Cosmetic work and painting must be agreed to in writing before move-in.

Rent and your portal

Activate your account and make a payment

First-time sign-in

Follow the activation link in your welcome email or text and create a password. You can also choose “Need to set up an account?” on the AppFolio sign-in page. Bookmark it or use the AppFolio app to see documents, pay rent, and request service.

When and how to pay

Rent is due on the first. Pay online by bank withdrawal, debit card, or credit card. You can also deliver a certified check or money order to the office. After the fifth, the portal stops accepting past-due payments. The handbook lists a $100 late fee if rent is unpaid by the sixth. Your deposit cannot serve as your last month’s rent.

Maintenance

Ask for a repair or reach the emergency line

Maintenance help ›

Routine service

Choose “Make a Maintenance Request” in your resident portal and describe the problem promptly so the team can coordinate with a vendor. Delayed reporting can leave you responsible for additional damage.

Emergency maintenance

Flooding, a gas leak, or loss of heat in winter are examples in the handbook. For immediate danger, get to safety and call 911. During office hours, call the office and submit a portal request. After hours, call (678) 782-7447 and leave your name, callback number, address, and what happened.

Locked out?

Contact a locksmith to regain entry. Tell management if the locks change so your home’s records can be updated.

Everyday home care

What to check, clean, and report

Monthly and seasonal checks

Test smoke and carbon monoxide detectors monthly and replace their batteries. Report a detector that still fails. Test GFCI outlets monthly. Change applicable furnace filters every three months and replace thermostat batteries as needed. Know where your breaker panel and main water shut-off are.

Water and moisture

Keep drains clear of hair, use a shower curtain or door, ventilate damp rooms, and report leaks, ceiling stains, or failed caulking promptly. Keep shut-offs accessible and the water heater clear. Leave gutter cleaning to the maintenance team.

Floors, fixtures, and appliances

Vacuum and spot-clean carpets, arrange professional steam cleaning as needed, and avoid wet-mopping wood or laminate. Use dishwasher detergent, protect counters from heat and cuts, and clean stove and hood filters as directed. Keep hard items out of the disposal and flush only toilet paper and human waste. Report failed appliances for repair approval.

Inside and outside

Use bulbs of the correct wattage. Report faulty window locks or deadbolts. Ask before mounting heavy items. Follow your lease for yard and pool care, report missed services or irrigation problems, and remove animal waste. Handle routine pest control other than rodents and follow local rules for hazardous waste.

Lease and house rules

Pets, guests, parking, and renewals

Read the pet policy ›

Pets and guests

Get owner approval and complete a PetScreening application before bringing a pet home. Monthly pet rent applies, and the handbook lists a $250 unauthorized-pet fine. Get approval for guests staying more than 14 days. Only approved occupants may live in the home. Contact management before any proposed occupancy change or lease assignment.

Parking, smoking, and HOA rules

Park approved, operational vehicles in designated areas. Commercial vehicles and smoking are prohibited. HOA fines caused by resident violations are billed as additional rent, with a $25 processing fee. The fee is waived if you show the violation was issued in error.

Renewing or ending your lease

Your agreement sets the term. The team reaches out about renewal 60 days before it ends. Contact your property manager about an extension or early termination. Submit move-out notice through the portal at least 30 days in advance.

Benefits and insurance

What the resident package includes

View benefits package details ›

Resident Benefit Package

The handbook lists the package at $35 per month. It includes HVAC filter delivery where applicable, resident rewards, credit-building through on-time rent payments, online account access, maintenance requests, a vetted vendor network, and a master insurance policy. Contact the Georgia team if you carry your own policy.

Coverage and deductibles

The handbook’s March 2024 master policy overview lists up to $100,000 in property damage liability and $10,000 for resident belongings, with a $500 belongings deductible. It also describes approved-pet liability up to $25,000, and separate limits for pet damage, mold, and bed bugs. Flood, wear and tear, hurricanes, and tornadoes are listed exclusions. Refer to your policy documents for the terms that apply to you.

Proof of insurance

The handbook says master-policy coverage begins at move-in, with evidence of insurance emailed about 30 days later.

Moving out

Leave the home ready for inspection

Before you leave

Submit notice in AppFolio, give the team your forwarding address, and take photos of the home. Keep utilities active for the inspection and for 72 hours after move-out. The handbook lists a $150 fee if utilities are disconnected early and a $75 re-key deduction.

Cleaning and repairs

Remove all possessions and trash. Have carpets professionally cleaned; the handbook lists a $250 deduction for skipping this step. Clean floors, windows, mirrors, bathrooms, kitchen appliances, and counters. Tidy landscaping and patios. Replace broken blinds, damaged doorstops, and burnt-out bulbs. Patch large holes, leave small nail holes, and leave paint matching to management. Restore unauthorized wall finishes.

Keys and deposit

Put house keys in the mounted lockbox. Leave garage remotes, mailbox keys, and amenity cards in the kitchen or a drawer. The team inspects within 24–72 hours of confirmed vacancy and emails its report within five to seven business days. The handbook gives a 30-day deposit refund window. Reply in writing to dispute a statement item.

STRAIGHT ANSWERS

Georgia resident FAQs

Find answers about your lease, repairs, and moving out.

16 questions

Move-out & deposits

When should I have the carpets cleaned?

Arrange professional cleaning within one month before move-out. Send the receipt before returning possession. Check your signed lease for the applicable deduction.

Move-out & deposits

When will I receive my deposit refund?

Confirm vacancy and provide a forwarding address. Expect an inspection within 24–72 hours, an emailed report in 5–7 business days, and any refund within 30 days. Reply to the report with itemized concerns.

Move-out & deposits

What can be deducted from my deposit?

Section 1.5 covers unpaid balances, damage beyond ordinary wear, re-keying, cleaning, and removal of belongings. Keep utilities connected for inspection. Your deposit cannot cover your final rent payment.

Ending a lease

How do I end my lease early?

For leases dated April 1, 2024 onward, submit at least 30 days’ formal notice. The termination fee is twice monthly rent. Pay one quarter with notice and the balance before moving out. Return the home rent-ready.

Ending a lease

Where are the early termination terms?

Review section 1.22 of your signed lease for eligibility, written notice, rent through termination, fees, and property condition.

Ending a lease

May I sublet my home?

Contact management before changing occupants. Your lease restricts subletting and assignment, and new residents require approval.

Ending a lease

Can the owner end my lease early?

Section 1.3 describes 60 days’ notice and a credit of one month’s rent after vacancy for early termination by the owner. Ordinary lease expiration is handled separately.

Maintenance

When should I call for emergency maintenance?

Call 911 for immediate danger. For urgent property damage such as flooding, call (678) 782-7447. During office hours, also submit a portal request. After hours, leave your address, callback number, and a description.

Maintenance

How do I request a routine repair?

Sign in to the resident portal and choose “Make a Maintenance Request” so the team can dispatch a vendor.

Occupants

How can someone join my lease?

Have the prospective adult occupant apply for screening. Email their full name to the Georgia team so management can prepare the updated agreement after approval.

Occupants

How can someone leave a shared lease?

The departing occupant submits formal notice and pays a $250 administrative fee. The remaining occupant provides an additional deposit equal to half a month’s rent.

Home transfers

Can I transfer near the end of my lease or month-to-month?

Give 30 days’ notice. Eligibility depends on the Georgia inspection ($125), payment history, and lease compliance. Budget for the new holding fee and deposit. Your existing deposit follows the regular move-out process.

Home transfers

Can I transfer during my lease?

The published transfer policy requires early termination, forfeiture of your current deposit, an inspection, and eligibility checks. Budget for a two-month termination fee, the new holding fee, and a new deposit. Ask your leasing agent to review the costs before giving notice.

Caring for the home

Can I paint or change fixtures?

Email the Georgia team photos, materials, and your timeline. Obtain written approval before beginning work. Section 1.25 explains workmanship and restoration requirements.

Caring for the home

Who maintains the lawn?

Check your lease and HOA services. Resident duties can include mowing, edging, weeding, beds, and hedges. Ask management about tree work and overgrown hedges above six feet.

Caring for the home

Can I install my own appliances?

Keep the original appliances safe and return them to their original positions at move-out. You are responsible for storage or transport damage. Obtain owner approval before leaving a replacement.

The “Fine Print” for the RBP for Prospective Renters

The All 3 Realty Resident Benefits Package (RBP) delivers savings and convenient, professional services that make taking care of your home second nature. By applying, applicant agrees to be enrolled and to pay the applicable cost of $35/month, payable with rent.

Your RBP may include, subject to property mechanicals or other limitations:

  • HVAC air filter delivery directly to your door approximately every 90 days
  • Liability insurance that meets all lease requirements from an A-rated carrier
  • A resident rewards program that helps you earn rewards for paying your rent on time
  • Credit building to help boost your credit score with timely rent payments
  • 24/7 online maintenance reporting
  • Home buying assistance for when the time is right to buy your “forever” home
  • Online portal that includes access to your account, documents, communication and payment options
  • Vetted vendor network: we find the technicians who are reputable, licensed, and insured

Liability insurance requirements and options:

The landlord requires tenant to obtain liability coverage of at least $100,000 in property damage and legal liability from an A-rated carrier and to maintain such coverage throughout the entire term of the lease agreement. Tenant is required to furnish landlord evidence of the required insurance prior to occupancy, at the time of each lease renewal period, and upon request. To satisfy the insurance requirement, tenant may either

  1. Be automatically enrolled into a policy that satisfies the coverage requirements as part of the Resident Benefits Package; or
  2. Obtain alternative liability coverage from an insurer of tenant’s choice. The option tenant chooses will not affect whether tenant’s lease application is approved or the terms of tenant’s lease.

Option 1: Do nothing. The tenant will be automatically enrolled into an insurance policy as part of the resident benefits package. No further action is required. Coverage will begin on the effective date of tenant’s lease and continue throughout the lease term. Please refer to the evidence of insurance that is supplied by All 3 Realty for additional coverage details.

Option 2: Buy a policy. If tenant prefers, tenant may find, purchase, and maintain another policy that satisfies the landlord’s requirements. The resident benefits package monthly amount will be adjusted accordingly. Visit http://insurance.residentforms.com/ and follow the instructions listed there to provide evidence of the required insurance coverage to your landlord. The resident benefits package monthly rate will be adjusted by the premium amount in the policy.

Please be sure that your policy meets the following criteria prior to submitting:

  • Policy is purchased from an A-rated carrier
  • Policy meets or exceeds the required $100,000 in property damage and personal liability
  • All 3 Realty is listed as additional interest
  • All 3 Realty address is listed as: PO Box 660121 Dallas, TX 75266

It is tenant’s responsibility to pay premiums directly to your insurance provider. If the policy is terminated or lapses, tenant will be subject to a lease violation fee of $25 and agrees to be subsequently enrolled into the policy referenced in option 1 above.

Note: The total monthly cost of the resident benefits package is all-inclusive, and no discounts will be given if any element of the package is unavailable due to a lack of HVAC or another limitation at a specific property. All services are subject to the terms and conditions of the resident benefits package lease addendum.
